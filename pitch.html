Nashville Tonight

Somebody's standing on Broadway
at 9pm. Nobody's telling them where to go.

The short version — 60 seconds, and I want your honest take at the end.

01The problem

There is no real directory for live music in downtown Nashville.

Google gives you a list of bars. It doesn't tell you that one room is traditional country with a fiddle and the one three doors down is a rock band with a cowboy hat on. Locals know. Tourists guess. Everybody ends up in the loudest place with the worst music, and the rooms that actually book real country don't get found.

02The product

One page. No app, no login. Scan the code, answer three questions:

1. What night are you out?Tonight, or plan the rest of the week.
2. What do you want to hear?Real country, classic country, bluegrass, roots, rock, two-step.
3. What kind of night is it?Sit and listen, honky tonk, dance floor — or a party bar, clearly labeled.

You get a short list of rooms, in walking distance, described honestly. Including a one-tap filter that hides the bachelorette scene — "skip the party bars." That's the whole promise: for people who came for the music.

03How people find it

Not an ad campaign. Physical objects in the places people already are — and a different object for every kind of hand it lands in.

  • Fridge magnets with a QR code — the backbone of it. Thousands of them. "More info →" and the code. Magnets get taken home, which is the point: locals keep it and use it every weekend.
  • Cards and flyers for the people who are only in town for two days — handed out at honky tonk doorways, boot shops, hotels.
  • Posters behind the bar and in the window of the rooms that want to be found.
  • Window stickers on the doors, so a walk down the street is the directory.
  • Every batch has its own code, so we know exactly which location and which hand produced the scans.

03bWho's actually handing it out

This is the part that makes the print budget work — it's not one guy with a stack of paper.

  • The bands hand them out. Every act playing the rooms we list gets magnets and cards for their own crowd. They're not doing us a favor — it's their show on the front, and it travels to the next gig and the next town.
  • Sponsoring bars carry all three — flyers at the door, posters inside, magnets on the bar. A bar that's on the page every week has a reason to keep the stack full.
  • Product and boot stores get placement. The shops where people already buy the boots and the hats — counter cards, window magnets, a stack by the register. That's the tourist hand-off, and it's the highest-intent traffic in the whole system.
  • Hotels and honky tonk doorways catch the 48-hour visitor who has no idea what's good.

What this is not

It does not become an advertising wall. Sponsors get a line and a logo, not the page. The directory stays editorial: a party bar is listed as a party bar whether they pay or not. The whole value is that it tells people what they actually want to do — which is why the print works in the first place.

Why magnets

Tourists leave in 48 hours. Locals live here and go out every weekend. A magnet on a fridge is repeat traffic; a flyer in a trash can is not.

Why not an app

Nobody installs an app to find a bar tonight. A QR code and a web page is zero friction and works on any phone in two seconds.

04Who it's for

Both directions. The same page serves the person who came for the music and the bachelorette party that came to go out — they just answer different questions.

Here for the music

Thirty-plus, tired of paying a cover to hear a rock band call itself country. They pick the music, pick the room, and tick skip the party bars. That's the promise the other guides don't make.

Here for the party

Bachelorette parties, birthdays, the group that wants a scene. Same filters, flipped: what kind of music, what age range they're comfortable in, what they're actually looking for — dance floor, live band, cocktails, big group — and what they want to spend on drinks and cover.

Nobody picks a bar because a flyer told them to. They pick it because it matches what they already wanted to do. That's the whole job: ask four quick questions, then answer honestly — including telling the bachelorette party which bar is actually fun for them, and telling the couple who drove in from Kentucky which room to avoid.

05How it makes money

  • Featured night — a venue pays $75/week to sit at the top of their night.
  • Partner — $450/month for every night they're open, plus their drink specials on the printed magnet/flyer.
  • Flyer/magnet sponsorship — a logo on the back of a print run, $250 per 1,000.
  • Drink specials — free to any venue, any tier. It's the easiest "yes" in the pitch and it's the reason people open the page twice.

Free listings stay free forever. Nobody can buy a better description — a party bar is listed as a party bar whether they pay or not. That's what makes the recommendations worth trusting.

"Nashville invented this music. We're just making sure people can find it on a Friday night."
